Dunning & suspension — reminders, grace, and what a suspended court can do

A daily job watches every subscription. It reminds courts before their paid period ends, gives a lapsed court a grace period, and, if the platform has suspension switched on, pauses creating and editing once grace runs out. Paying restores the court. Every box shows the endpoint it calls (base path /api/v1).

Things worth knowing

  • The rules are one platform-wide set. Until a Platform Admin saves them the defaults apply: first reminder 14 days out, final reminder 3 days out, 3 days of grace, 3 automatic retries, suspension on, auto-reactivate on, a reason required on every override.
  • Grace restricts nothing. It is a warning window. Only suspended pauses creating, editing and deleting, and only on cases, casefiles, evidence, schedules, comments and uploads. Reading and downloading are never restricted.
  • A court that has never subscribed has no status and is never restricted. Platform Admins are never blocked.
  • Renewing early or late restores the court at once. If auto-reactivate is switched off, the payment is still recorded and the period extended, but the court stays suspended until an admin sets it back to active.
  • With suspension switched off, a court stays in grace however long it goes unpaid.
  • A manual subscription moves to grace the moment its period ends. An automatic one is given a day for its charge to land before it does.
  • Reminders go to every active Judge, Registrar and Legal Aide with an email, once per paid period for each of the two reminders. A reminder that could not be delivered is retried the next day. The email is honest about the pause: it only mentions grace and suspension when suspension is on.
  • To waive a cycle's fee, use the invoice waive in Platform subscriptions; find the invoice with the attempts view.
  • The court subscription design shows the read-only banner from the day a subscription expires. The approved rules here wait until grace runs out. Moving the line is a one-line change; it is not decided yet.
